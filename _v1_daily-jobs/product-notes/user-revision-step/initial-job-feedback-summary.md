# Initial job-feedback summary

Captured: 2026-08-05

Source: user-provided review of an initial set of job postings, including copied
positive and negative excerpts and pass/fail verdicts.

Purpose: product-research evidence for a possible post-onboarding calibration step.
This document is descriptive, not an approved update to the active search profile.

## Relevant background shared by the user

- The user is building Daily Jobs: collecting company information, improving job
  discovery with prompts and AI-assisted web research, and plotting jobs or
  companies on a map.
- The user is also building a rapid-prototyping tool for product and frontend
  engineers using HTML, CSS, and JSON snapshots. They see rapid prototypes as an
  early step in AI-assisted product engineering and as evidence of 0-to-1 thinking.
- The user lived in Seoul for seven years and worked in online education.

## Emerging positive signals

The feedback suggests interest in roles that combine several of these qualities:

- Product-oriented frontend or full-stack work with strong design instincts.
- High standards for UX craft, interaction details, accessibility, and empathy.
- 0-to-1 work where product shape and success criteria are still evolving.
- Rapid UI prototyping and thoughtful use of AI development tools.
- Building AI-native or LLM-powered product experiences across the stack.
- Direct customer collaboration and ownership of product decisions, not only
  implementation from fixed specifications.
- Agent, system, and workflow thinking tied to real customer outcomes.
- Domain overlap with consulting, language learning, Korea, or education.

## Emerging negative or caution signals

- Team pages dominated by prestigious large-technology-company backgrounds may
  feel less welcoming, even when the role responsibilities are highly appealing.
- Language associated with "10x engineers," relentless multiplication, "no
  excuses," or aggressively blunt communication can read as insufficiently human
  or potentially high-pressure.
- A founding title and very small company increase perceived execution and company
  failure risk, although this was a concern rather than an automatic rejection.
- Vague postings reduce confidence because they provide fewer specific points of
  personal alignment, but vagueness alone did not cause rejection in this sample.
- A role outside San Francisco remains a clear rejection regardless of other fit.
- Missing culture and team evidence is an uncertainty worth investigating, not
  automatically a negative verdict.

## Product-research hypotheses

These hypotheses may be useful in a future calibration experiment, but they are not
approved profile changes:

1. Work-content fit is necessary but can be overridden by culture and team signals.
2. Product craft, design judgment, rapid prototyping, and customer proximity are
   stronger positive signals than generic AI-company affiliation.
3. Prestige-heavy team composition may be a negative culture signal for this user.
4. Humane, sustainable cultural language matters; intensity-coded language can
   outweigh otherwise strong technical fit.
5. Relevant lived and industry experience may increase interest, but the user also
   cares whether employers will recognize it as credible engineering context.
6. Early-stage risk is tolerated selectively rather than prohibited.
7. Location remains a hard gate, while vague descriptions and missing culture data
   are confidence penalties rather than automatic exclusions.

## Gaps and ambiguities to preserve

- The reason for Gem's fail verdict is missing.
- Speak's concern about domain experience and engineering credibility is not yet
  clear enough to encode.
- The review uses "pass" to mean a positive verdict and "fail" to mean rejection;
  a future interface should use less ambiguous labels such as `pursue` and `skip`.
- The reviewed set is small and not representative enough to justify automatic
  profile changes.
